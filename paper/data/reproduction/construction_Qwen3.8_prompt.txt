Look at this construction-site image and report every clearly visible violation from the four categories below in one JSON answer. Most categories will usually be absent in a single image. A dangerous-looking construction scene is not enough by itself; mark a category true only when its specific target and violation are visible.

rule_1_ppe_violation means a worker on foot is visibly missing required personal protection. A clearly bare head is a violation. A worker who visibly wears a hard hat should normally be treated as compliant unless another required item is unmistakably missing for the task shown; ordinary work clothes or the absence of a reflective vest in ordinary daylight are not enough by themselves. Do not guess about small, distant, blurred, or hidden clothing, footwear, eyes, or faces. Put a tight box around each violating worker's visible body, not around the missing item.

rule_2_fall_protection_violation means a worker is clearly about three metres or more above a lower level, is exposed because effective edge protection is absent, and is not using a safety harness. Workers on the ground, on a low platform or dirt slope, behind effective guardrails, or at an uncertain height are not violations. Box each exposed worker, not the structure or edge.

rule_3_unprotected_edge_violation means an accessible upper edge borders a visibly deep excavation or steep drop of about three metres or more and lacks guardrails, fencing, or warning protection. The substantial height difference and the hazardous upper edge must both be discernible in the image rather than inferred merely because excavation work or exposed soil is present. Soil piles, excavation walls without an accessible upper edge, shallow trenches, gentle slopes, ordinary uneven ground, distant cuts with uncertain depth, and protected edges are not violations. Put one box around each continuous hazardous upper edge and only enough adjacent drop to show the hazard; do not box workers or the whole image.

rule_4_excavator_proximity_violation means a visible worker is inside the normal reach of an excavator's rear counterweight, rotating body, boom, or bucket. A still photograph need not show actual motion: count a worker who is clearly within the machine's ordinary swing or travel zone, including directly beside its rear or tracks in the active work area. Do not count the cab operator, a worker behind a barrier, a worker separated by a clearly safe gap, or simple distant co-appearance. Box each endangered worker, not the excavator or a combined worker-machine area.

A visible event is {"present":true,"reason":"short visible evidence","boxes":[[x1,y1,x2,y2]]}; an absent or uncertain event is {"present":false,"reason":null,"boxes":[]}.
